# Track 7: Product Validation and Commercialization Plan

> **Planning-only hold:** Do not run external interviews, offers, pricing tests, or commercialization work until the operator repeatedly uses and accepts the relevant Arda slice. Current-source competitive research may be planned later; do not delegate by default.

**Goal:** Determine who values an ambient governed personal-agent system, which outcome they will pay for, and what product/support boundary can be offered without weakening the operator-first vision.

**Architecture:** This track begins only after a real operator-used slice exists and consumes that behavior rather than speculative descriptions. It separates competitive novelty, user desirability, technical feasibility, operational support cost, and willingness to pay. Product decisions are evidence dispositions governed by Varda, not marketing claims produced by implementation agents.

**Methods:** Current competitive research, structured interviews, observed prototype sessions, willingness-to-pay experiments, privacy/security review, support-cost measurement, and bounded private-alpha offers.

---

## Core hypotheses—not claims

1. A segment of technically ambitious operators wants one persistent agent relationship across phone, workstation, room displays, and devices.
2. Continuity, inspectability, privacy, and governed physical embodiment are more valuable to that segment than a generic voice assistant or dashboard.
3. Mirromere's embodied avatar and RELIC's truthful runtime visualization make useful behavior easier to access and understand rather than merely decorating unavailable capabilities.
4. A staged software-first product can produce value before custom mirror/RELIC hardware exists.
5. Some users will pay for setup, integration, support, or hardware even if the core software remains local-first.

Every hypothesis can be rejected, narrowed, or split by evidence.

## Task 1: Produce a current competitive landscape

**Artifact:** `docs/research/ambient-agent-competitive-landscape.md`

**Research lanes:**

- general agent runtimes and multimodal assistants;
- persistent memory/context products;
- phone-to-desktop continuity;
- smart-home/ambient voice systems;
- premium privacy-first home assistants;
- agent observability/visualization;
- wearable presence/identity systems;
- robots and fabrication-device orchestration;
- two-way mirror and spatial display products;
- open-source personal automation platforms.

**Steps:**
1. Run current web research with primary product documentation, release notes, demos, pricing, privacy terms, and shipping status.
2. Record date, source, claimed capability, observed availability, authority/privacy model, hardware dependence, and evidence quality.
3. Distinguish announced, demoed, beta, shipping, integrated, and independently verified.
4. Build a capability matrix against Arda's proposed combination: Hermes-centered continuity, governed memory/research, source-truth HUD, Mirromere, RELIC, presence, and typed outposts.
5. Search patents and academic systems only as a separate lane; legal conclusions require counsel.
6. Ask Varda to disposition: direct competitor, adjacent substitute, component supplier, inspiration, partnership candidate, or weakly related.
7. State the narrowest defensible differentiation. Never publish “nobody else is doing this” unless the defined claim and search evidence support it.

## Task 2: Define initial user profiles and jobs-to-be-done

**Artifact:** `docs/research/ambient-agent-user-profiles.md`

Start with profiles aligned to the product rather than broad consumer assumptions:

- technical founder/researcher with many concurrent projects;
- accessibility/executive-function power user who needs continuity;
- maker with local compute, displays, printers, sensors, or robots;
- privacy-conscious self-hoster who rejects cloud-only ambient assistants;
- creative professional who values spatial/visual agent presence;
- premium smart-home user seeking deeper agent continuity.

For each profile document:

- current workaround and failure cost;
- desired outcome, not requested feature;
- data sensitivity and trust requirement;
- installation/support tolerance;
- must-have surface/device;
- unacceptable behavior;
- purchase actor and plausible budget source;
- evidence from interviews/observation versus project hypothesis.

Do not use medical or neurodivergent identity as a marketing shortcut. Accessibility benefits must be tested respectfully and never imply diagnosis or treatment.

## Task 3: Create demo protocols tied to technical slices

**Artifact:** `docs/operations/ambient-agent-demo-protocols.md`

Create one bounded demo per proven slice:

1. **Ignition:** restart → click icon → health → HUD.
2. **Continuity:** phone conversation → desktop `Continue here`.
3. **Embodiment:** a sustained text/voice exchange with the Mirromere avatar plus one real context-resume or Personal Operations action; no dashboard, radar, or staged status scene.
4. **Arrival:** enrolled presence moves an already-working Mirromere from passive/private behavior to a safe greeting and back on departure.
5. **Runtime visibility:** genuine delegation visible on RELIC.
6. **Physical action:** approved printer simulation, then attended real device.

Each protocol must define setup, participant consent, data retained, facilitator script, success/failure observations, recovery, and a prohibition on hiding degraded states. Do not demo unproven automation with staged “live” labels.

## Task 4: Run problem interviews before pricing claims

**Artifact:** private, redacted interview evidence plus `docs/research/ambient-agent-interview-synthesis.md`

**Steps:**
1. Recruit outside the immediate project feedback loop and across several candidate profiles.
2. Ask about recent real events: losing context, switching surfaces, managing agents, home arrival, device workflows, privacy failures, and support burden.
3. Avoid leading with “JARVIS” until the participant describes current needs; then show the smallest relevant demo.
4. Capture behavior, current spending, alternatives, objections, trust thresholds, and requested proof.
5. Separate excitement from commitment: follow-up, installation willingness, data access, pilot time, referral, deposit, or paid setup.
6. Have Varda synthesize repeated jobs, disconfirming evidence, and profile splits with source references.

## Task 5: Test willingness to pay with bounded offers

Do not begin with a mass-market subscription. Test offers matched to actual support burden:

- paid design-partner installation;
- private-alpha software/support membership;
- premium workstation + Mirromere setup service;
- managed local appliance or outpost kit;
- enterprise/research lab embodiment and observability package;
- open core plus paid integration/support, if governance and sustainability align.

**Steps:**
1. Measure the real labor, hardware, hosted-model, support, and replacement cost of each technical slice.
2. Draft offer pages that state what is genuinely proven and what remains experimental.
3. Test a small number of explicit prices/offers with qualified participants; record acceptance, rejection, and reason.
4. Prefer refundable design-partner deposits or signed pilot commitments over survey enthusiasm when appropriate and lawful.
5. Keep consequential billing/payment outside autonomous agent authority.
6. Do not select a permanent business model until repeated use and support cost are known.

## Task 6: Validate trust, privacy, and safety as product value

**Artifacts:** threat-model and privacy review linked from the technical phase evidence.

Test whether users understand and value:

- local processing and raw-sensor retention limits;
- physical camera/microphone disable;
- presence versus identity versus authorization;
- personal/business partitioning;
- inspectable source freshness and degraded states;
- approval before physical or external consequential action;
- export, deletion, revocation, and uninstall.

Record where safety adds confidence and where it adds friction. Simplify interaction, not authority boundaries.

## Task 7: Establish private-alpha entry and exit gates

A participant enters only when:

- their intended slice is technically proven on the supported profile;
- setup, backup, rollback, and support ownership are explicit;
- collected data and retention are consented;
- hardware/sensor risks are disclosed;
- no unsupported autonomous physical action is implied.

A product slice advances when:

- participants use it repeatedly without facilitation;
- the intended job improves measurably or observably;
- failures are recoverable and honestly represented;
- support burden is sustainable for the offer;
- at least one meaningful commitment exceeds verbal enthusiasm;
- privacy/safety objections have a bounded disposition.

## Commercial evidence ladder

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Interest | person expresses curiosity/excitement |
| Problem evidence | person describes recent costly behavior/workaround |
| Demo value | person completes a relevant scenario and asks to reuse it |
| Pilot commitment | person commits time, environment access, or a written pilot |
| Paid commitment | person pays/deposits for a bounded offer |
| Repeated use | person uses the slice without facilitation over time |
| Retention/referral | person continues, expands, or refers another qualified user |
| Scalable offer | delivery/support cost and reliability support broader sale |

Do not collapse these levels into “demand.”

## Branch and data boundary

Use `research/ambient-product-validation` for public, source-backed research artifacts. Keep participant identities, recordings, contact details, private environment data, and commercial correspondence outside the public repository. Only redacted synthesis and consent-safe metrics enter tracked docs. This track does not edit implementation paths except through reviewed product requirements returned to the program coordinator.

## Track gate

The project may claim a defensible differentiated product direction after current competitive research and repeated problem evidence. It may claim monetary demand only after real commitments. It may claim a scalable business only after repeated use, support-cost evidence, and successful bounded delivery. Until then, novelty and monetary value remain promising hypotheses worth testing—not facts to market as proven.
