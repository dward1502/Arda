I was $40,000 in debt with a family counting on me and a day job that paid just enough to keep me stuck.

No investors. No connections. No trust fund safety net.

Just a laptop and an obsession with one idea that everyone around me thought was stupid: if I build an audience, I build everything.

Four years later I run a content agency. I have 365,000+ followers. An 84,000-subscriber newsletter. Clients who find me because they already trust me before the first call ever happens. I never raised a dollar.

Yes, I run ads. But ads did not build this business. Distribution did. The ads work because the brand exists. Not the other way around. The content created the trust. The ads just amplified what was already there.

Take away my ad budget tomorrow and the inbound still flows. Take away my distribution and the ads become the most expensive way to talk to strangers.

That is not a marketing insight. That is an equity position. And distribution is the most undervalued asset in business right now.

Let me explain why.

## 

The Old Playbook Has a Hole in It

Founders still think equity means cap tables, stock options, and ownership stakes in a company.

That definition made sense in 2010. It does not make sense in 2026.

Here is what happened while everyone was focused on raising rounds:

B2B customer acquisition costs surged 222% in eight years. 60% in the last five alone. The average B2B SaaS company now spends $702 to acquire a single customer. Fintech companies spend $1,461. Enterprise deals cost $14,772 per closed customer.

Global social media ad spend hit $277 billion. The spend keeps growing but the returns are not keeping pace. Only 28% of marketers report positive ROI from X ads. The cost to reach people is rising every quarter.

Meanwhile, 60% of all Google searches now end without a single click. 80% when AI Overviews show up. The traditional path from ad spend to website visit to conversion is getting thinner every month.

Ads still work. I run them. But here is what most founders get wrong. They treat ads as the strategy instead of the amplifier. They are spending money to introduce themselves to strangers over and over again instead of building something that makes strangers come to them.

Ads on top of distribution is leverage. Ads instead of distribution is a money pit. That is the gap nobody is talking about.

## 

The New Equity

Naval Ravikant said it years ago. "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity."

Everyone quotes him. Almost nobody understands what he actually meant.

He was not just talking about stock in a company. He was talking about leverage. Ownership of an asset that compounds without requiring your time for every dollar earned.

Content is that asset. An audience is that equity.

Think about it.

Morning Brew built a newsletter to 2.5 million subscribers. Sold a majority stake for $75 million. The Hustle built a newsletter to 1.5 million subscribers. HubSpot bought it for $27 million. Not because of their revenue model. Because of their distribution. HubSpot realized it was cheaper to buy an audience than to keep spending $25 million a month trying to reach one through SEO and ads.

The Hustle was acquiring subscribers for less than $2 each. HubSpot was spending hundreds per lead through traditional channels. The math was not even close.

This is not a media trend. This is a valuation event.

When someone owns the direct relationship with the audience, they own the most liquid asset in business. More liquid than inventory. More liquid than IP. Because attention converts to anything. Products. Services. Partnerships. Funding. Talent.

You cannot say that about a warehouse full of product nobody knows exists.

## 

Why Founders Specifically

Here is where most people get this wrong. They think distribution means hiring a social media manager to post quotes on the company page.

That is not distribution. That is decoration.

The data is brutal on this. Employee-shared posts outperform brand content by 5 to 10x. Individual accounts get 8x more engagement than corporate channels. LinkedIn's algorithm gives personal posts 39% of feed visibility. Company pages get 1 to 2%.

Airbnb runs every feature release through Brian Chesky's personal account. OpenAI's biggest engagement during the Sora launch came from Sam Altman's tweets, not the company channel. Cursor built a $300 million ARR business partly because the co-founders were visible and accessible online.

People trust people. They do not trust logos.

73% of B2B buyers consider thought leadership a more trustworthy indicator of competence than traditional marketing materials. 75% of business leaders said a piece of thought leadership led them to research a product they were not previously considering. 60% said it made them realize they were missing a significant business opportunity.

And here is the one that should keep every silent founder up at night: 25% of B2B buyers who encountered a competitor's thought leadership ended or significantly reduced their relationship with their current vendor.

Your competitor's LinkedIn post is stealing your clients while you are too busy to write one.

## 

The Compound Effect Nobody Talks About

Here is the difference between ads and distribution. When I turn off an ad campaign, the leads stop. When I stop posting for a week, the DMs keep coming. A post I wrote six months ago still generates inbound today. A thread I published last year still gets bookmarked every week.

That article, "AI + Personal Brand = The Future," hit 269,000 views. People are still sharing it. Still quoting it. Still finding me through it. I did not spend a dollar to promote it.

That is not a post. That is a performing asset.

And this is where the equity comparison becomes literal. Equity compounds over time. So does distribution.

The first 30 days of posting feel like nothing is happening. Day 60 you start seeing patterns. Day 90 the inbound starts. By day 180 you have a flywheel that generates opportunities while you sleep.

Now imagine running ads into that flywheel. That is where the real math changes.

Organic content leads convert at rates 30 to 50% higher than paid leads. In B2B SaaS, organic LinkedIn content generates leads at $164 each. LinkedIn ads cost $310 for the same lead. But when someone clicks your ad and lands on a profile with 300 posts, a clear thesis, and proof everywhere they look, your conversion rate is not the industry average. It is multiples higher. The distribution makes every ad dollar work harder.

Ads without distribution is paying full price for cold attention. Ads with distribution is buying warm attention at a discount. Every post you have ever published lowers your effective CAC on every campaign you will ever run.

## 

The Architecture vs. Masonry Problem

Most founders treat content like masonry. Lay a brick. Lay another brick. Hope something holds.

That is why they quit after two weeks. There is no blueprint. No system. Just random acts of posting.

Distribution as equity requires architecture.

You need a thesis. A point of view the market can organize around. Not tips. Not motivation. A position that forces people to pick a side.

Mine was simple: the founder is the brand. Not the company. Not the logo. The human being. And the ones who build distribution will own the next decade.

That one idea generated every client, every partnership, every opportunity I have had in the last four years. I did not need a hundred ideas. I needed one that was true enough to build everything on top of.

Dan Koe calls it the trust matrix. Growth, authenticity, authority. Three pillars that compound when you get them right.

Naval called it specific knowledge combined with leverage and accountability.

I call it simpler than both of them. Say what you actually believe. Say it in public. Say it often enough that people cannot think about the topic without thinking about you.

That is not personal branding. That is building equity in the attention economy.

## 

What This Means for the Next Five Years

Here is where the gap becomes permanent.

AI is making search irrelevant for discovery. 80-85% of queries now resolve inside the search interface without a single click to a website. Google is eating its own ecosystem. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini are answering questions that used to drive traffic to your website.

The founders who have distribution, who have audiences that trust them, who have direct email relationships with 10,000 or 100,000 people, those founders do not need Google. They do not need the algorithm. They have a direct line to the people who buy from them.

The founders who built everything on SEO and paid ads with no owned audience underneath are going to wake up one morning and realize the pipeline is thinner than it used to be. Not because they stopped spending. Because the infrastructure they built on belongs to someone else.

I am not saying stop running ads. I am saying stop running them into a void. Build the distribution first. Then let the ads pour gasoline on it.

Companies with recognized founder brands show 15 to 20% higher acquisition multiples. CEO thought leadership can yield 14x ROI, with customer acquisition costs dropping up to 40%.

That is the premium the market puts on distribution. Not potential distribution. Not planned distribution. Actual, existing, compounding relationships between a founder and their audience.

## 

Five Steps to Start Building Distribution Equity This Week

I am not going to give you theory. I am going to give you what actually works. Someone can read this tonight and execute tomorrow morning.

1. Pick your thesis. One sentence. Not your industry. Not your product. Your belief about where the world is heading and why most people are wrong about it. Mine: "The founder is the brand. Everything else is infrastructure." Write yours down. That sentence is your north star for every piece of content.

2. Post your origin story. The real one. Not the polished version. The one with the debt, the doubt, the moment you decided to bet on yourself anyway. Identity-driven content outperforms tactical content every time because people remember who you are before they remember what you know.

3. Build in public for 30 days. Share one decision, one lesson, or one observation from running your business every single day. Short posts. Two to three sentences. No fluff. The goal is not to go viral. The goal is to become familiar. Familiarity builds trust faster than brilliance.

4. Start a newsletter. Even if it is 100 subscribers. Those 100 people are yours. Not the algorithm's. Not the platform's. Yours. Paid newsletter subscriptions on Beehiiv alone generated $19 million for creators in 2025, up 138% from the year before. The median time to first dollar dropped to 66 days. Owned distribution is the only kind that cannot be taken from you.

5. Treat every post like a deposit. You are not creating content. You are making deposits into an asset that compounds. Every post adds to your library. Every share extends your reach. Every DM is a potential client, partner, hire, or investor. Stop thinking about content as a task. Start thinking about it as a balance sheet.

## 

The Real Question

Every founder understands the concept of equity. You would never give away 100% of your company because you understand that ownership is where the wealth lives.

So why are you giving away 100% of your distribution?

Every dollar you spend on ads without distribution behind it disappears when the campaign ends. You own nothing. But every dollar you spend on ads when people already know your name, already trust your content, already follow your thinking? That dollar works three times harder. Every month you stay silent, a competitor is building the audience that should have been yours.

Distribution is not marketing. Distribution is ownership. It compounds. It appreciates. It converts to revenue, talent, partnerships, and exit multiples.

I built an agency from nothing because I understood one thing before most people did. The person with the audience does not need to ask permission. They do not need to cold call. And when they do spend money on ads, every dollar goes further because the trust already exists.

They already have it.

The question is not whether distribution is the new equity.

The question is how much more of it you are willing to give away before you start building yours.
