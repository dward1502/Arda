How to automate TikTok Shop and YouTube with ChatGPT 5.5 and make $6,000-10,000/month

A high schooler with a phone and a $20 ChatGPT subscription is making $6,000-10,000/month TikTok and YouTube. No camera, no face, no editor.

This isn't an exception. It's a system that runs on an old laptop at home - and AI does 80% of the work.

YouTube pays $2,000-10,000 per million views. TikTok Shop top creators make $10,000-100,000 a month from affiliate alone. None of them film themselves on camera.

Here's the exact system - from first video to $4,000+ a month.

First - the money, because most people count wrong

Everyone asks how much TikTok pays per view. That's the wrong question.

```text
TikTok per views:
1,000 views      → $0.80-3.50
1,000,000 views  → $800-3,000
Top content      → up to $11,000 per million

YouTube per views:
1,000 views      → $4-15 (RPM)
1,000,000 views  → $4,000-15,000
Finance/SaaS     → $20-50 RPM
```

But real money doesn't come from views. A typical TikTok creator making $10,000 a month earns 60% from brand deals, 15% from Creator Rewards, 15% from lives and donations and only 10% from affiliate. Views are traffic. Money comes from sales.

That's where TikTok Shop comes in.

## 

Part 1 - TikTok Shop: where the real money is

TikTok Shop affiliate means you film a video about someone else's product and collect a commission on every sale. A video with 10,000-50,000 views can outsell a video with a million if it reaches the right audience.

```text
Average TikTok Shop affiliate:        $1000-6,000/month
Top creators from affiliate alone:    $16,000+/month
Records:                              $80,000-150,000/month
```

AI automates the entire video creation process. You pick a product - AI writes the script, generates the hook, builds the caption and hashtags. All that's left is to record or assemble from existing clips.

## 

Part 2 - YouTube Shorts and faceless channels

YouTube pays significantly more per view than TikTok. In the right niche RPM reaches $20-50 - meaning a million views can bring $20,000-50,000. But it requires niche focus and consistency.

Most profitable niches for faceless channels: finance and investing, animal and nature facts, motivation and psychology, top lists and rankings, technology and AI, horror stories.

The full pipeline from idea to finished video:

```text

→ Publish
```

Real automation is not "ask ChatGPT to write a script." It's a pipeline where a product goes in at the start and finished content for every platform comes out at the end.


## 

Part 4 - How to find the right product: AI analyzer instead of guessing

Most beginners grab the first product they find and start filming. Then wonder why there are no sales. The problem isn't the video - it's the product.

A good TikTok Shop product has seven criteria: it can be demonstrated visually, it solves an obvious pain, it's priced for impulse buying, it has a clear before/after, it can be explained in 3 seconds, it already shows up in TikTok Creative Center trends and it isn't boring on screen.

Before filming anything - run this prompt:

```text
Act as a TikTok Shop product analyst.

Analyze this product: [product]

Score from 1-10:
1. Visual demo potential
2. Pain point clarity
3. Impulse-buy potential
4. Before/after potential
5. UGC potential
6. Trend fit
7. Competition risk

Then give:
- best target audience
- 5 content angles
- 10 hooks
- final verdict: shoot / skip / test with 3 videos
```

If the product scores under 6 on most criteria - don't waste your time. AI tells you this in 30 seconds instead of you figuring it out after 20 filmed videos.

The free tool nobody uses: TikTok Creative Center shows top products, trending hashtags, top performing ads and what people are buying right now. Check it before every new product before you film a single second.

Part 5 - First 10 videos: the structure that sells

Don't know what to film - here are 10 formats that work for any product:

```text
1.  Problem → solution
```

Run it once - get a two-week content plan.

## 

Part 6 - Mistakes that cost the first 3 months

```text
1. Choosing a product that can't be shown visually
2. Starting the video with an intro instead of a hook
3. Filming 1-2 videos and waiting for sales
4. Not testing 10-20 different angles for one product
5. Copying a trend without connecting it to your product
6. Not checking TikTok Creative Center before filming
7. Not building a system — posting randomly
```

The most expensive mistake is number 3. The algorithm doesn't promote videos because they're good. It promotes videos people don't skip. Finding that video requires filming at least 20-30 variations and looking at the numbers.

The formula to remember:

> Hook → Retention → Rewatch → Click → Buy.

TikTok and YouTube don't promote the best video. They promote the video people watch all the way through.
